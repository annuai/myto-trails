# 14 — Platform contract: web, Trails app, Myto Navi

Specification of how the three clients share one backend. Planning only. Items marked **(open)** depend on facts not yet known (mostly Navi's capabilities).

## 1. The three clients

| Client | Who builds it | Interface | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Web** (`moto-trails.com`) | Trails project | Touch/desktop browser | Discover, contribute, moderate |
| **Trails companion app** | Trails project (companion stage) | Touch phone app (iOS and Android) | Records rides in the background (even while Google Maps or another navigation app is in the foreground), trims and cleans them, adds waypoints and photos, submits GPX and details as a draft |
| **Myto Navi** | Navi team | Android hardware with physical controls, no touch-first UI, has a SIM | Reads trails and GPX for navigation, works offline, can record rides, can report conditions from the field |

Trails is the system of record for trail data. Navi and the app are clients of it. All three submit through the same pipeline and the same moderation queue.

## 2. Principles
1. **One API, one pipeline.** An app- or Navi-recorded ride is a `submission` like any other; it is never auto-published, and it never creates verified access information (a recording shows a route was ridden, not that it is legal).
2. **Offline first, sync later.** Trails often have no signal; clients queue work and reconcile by revision numbers and idempotency keys.
3. **Access travels with the file.** A GPX without its access class, verification date and permission notice is dangerous offline, so exports embed them.
4. **Responsible riding on every surface.** Any client that shows Trails content shows its access and condition information alongside it. Restricted, private or unverified trails are never presented as open.
5. **Private by default.** Raw recordings are personal location data, private until the rider submits.

## 3. API

- **Base:** `https://moto-trails.com/api/v1`, JSON, described by an OpenAPI document; typed client in `packages/api-client`.
- **Auth:** the app uses the website's email-link sign-in followed by an authorisation-code exchange with PKCE for a device token ([ADR 0007](adr/0007-app-sign-in-through-the-website.md)); original plan: OAuth 2.0 authorisation code + PKCE for the app; revocable **device tokens** (hashed, scoped, listed on the user's profile page) for Navi; short-lived access tokens with refresh. Device identity never relies on the SIM.
- **Scopes (examples):** `read:trails`, `read:saved`, `write:saved`, `write:submission`, `write:report`.
- **Resources (initial):**
  - `GET /trails?near=…&radius=…&filters…`, `GET /trails/{public_id}` — only what the public site shows.
  - `GET /trails/{public_id}/gpx` — the sanitised export (§4), same access rules as the web.
  - `GET /me/saved`, `PUT/DELETE /me/saved/{public_id}`.
  - `POST /uploads` — returns a signed, resumable upload URL to object storage; client uploads directly.
  - `POST /submissions` (draft), `PATCH /submissions/{id}`, `POST /submissions/{id}/submit`; `GET /submissions/{id}` for status and moderator feedback.
  - `POST /trails/{public_id}/reports` — closure and other reports (field reports from Navi use this).
  - `GET /sync?since=…` — changes to Saved trails (see §6).
- **Idempotency:** every create carries a client-generated `client_id` (UUID); repeating a request returns the original result.
- **Versioning:** additive changes within `v1`; breaking changes need `v2`. Deprecation notice period documented.
- **Limits:** per-user and per-device rate limits; large uploads are chunked and resumable.
- **Public DTOs only:** responses are built by the same query layer as the web, so unpublished content, evidence files and permission contacts can never appear (doc 10 §5).

## 4. GPX export format (what Navi and other GPS apps receive)

Goal: open reliably on a device whose GPX capabilities are not yet known **(open)**, so use the lowest-risk subset.
- **GPX 1.1**, UTF-8, one `<trk>` with one or more `<trkseg>`; elevations in `<ele>` where available; timestamps omitted (or minimal) to avoid leaking rider data.
- A small number of ordinary `<wpt>` points (start, end, named points of interest) with `<name>` and short `<desc>`.
- **No custom extensions** required to read the file.
- **Metadata in standard fields:** `<metadata><name>` = trail name; `<desc>` = access class and condition at export, e.g. "Access: Permission required · Condition: Seasonal · Verified 14 Aug 2026 (re-check by Feb 2027) · Read before riding: https://moto-trails.com/trails/{slug}"; `<author>`, `<copyright>`/licence and a `<link>` to the trail page.
- Coordinates rounded to 5 decimals (~1 m); sensitive trails export only what their `public_precision` allows.
- Regenerated by our own writer on request; the original upload is never served.
- **Acceptance test (open):** the file opens and displays correctly on a real Navi unit, plus two other GPS apps. Until Navi's supported features are known, manual download and transfer is the supported path.

## 5. Trails app: recording and submission

**Recording**
- Background location while another app is foreground: Android foreground service with persistent notification; iOS "Always" location with background mode. **Biggest technical risk:** Indian-market Android phones (Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo, Samsung and others) aggressively kill background services; mitigated by the foreground service, in-app guidance for battery-optimisation exemptions, incremental crash-safe writes (points flushed to local storage every few seconds), and automatic resume. Validated first by a spike on real budget phones (roadmap M10).
- Pause/resume, auto-pause detection, accuracy filtering, smoothing, barometer elevation where present, battery-aware sampling.
- Waypoints during the ride (gate, water crossing, hazard, viewpoint) with optional photo.
- Works with no signal; uploads queue and resume.

**Cleaning before submit**
- Trim handles for the road to and from the trailhead and for **privacy** (home start/end); the trim is recorded in `trim_applied`.
- Preview map, distance, elevation profile, moving vs stopped time.

**Submission**
- The app creates a draft submission through the API: GPX, waypoints/photos, name, summary, terrain, difficulty, shape, access class and evidence (same fields and rules as the web form; riders can finish on the web).
- Responsible-riding reminder and the permission interstitial wording are identical to the web.
- Recordings and drafts stay private until submitted; the rider can delete any of them; the app shows what is stored on the server.

## 6. Myto Navi integration

Navi is a separate product with its own touch-free UI. Trails provides the data contract; the Navi team builds the app.

| Level | What | When |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Manual | Download the GPX on web or phone and transfer to Navi | Web MVP |
| 2. Share | "Send to Navi" share intent / deep link from the trail page | Companion stage |
| 3. Account sync | Navi signs in as a device; pulls Saved trails and offline packs (GPX + status snapshot + thumbnail); `content_revision` per trail tells it when a copy is stale; refreshes status over its SIM when connected | Companion stage |
| 4. Field reports | Navi sends closure and condition reports and recorded rides through the same endpoints | Companion stage |

- **Offline:** Navi stores trails with their access status and verification date and shows them on screen; when connected, `GET /sync?since=…` returns changed trails (closed, access changed, verification expired). Data volumes are small; deltas keep SIM costs low.
- **Offline maps:** self-hosted OpenStreetMap-derived vector tiles are the clean route (doc 05 §7); commercial tile providers' offline-caching terms are unverified.
- **Signal reality:** the SIM helps on roads and in towns, not deep in forests; everything must degrade to offline.
- **Navi-recorded rides** are `source = navi_recorded` with device info; they enter the same moderation queue.

## 7. Data-licence requirement
Contributors must grant a licence that explicitly covers redistribution to Myto products, including Navi and offline copies, **before launch**; changing it later is hard (D-8, doc 13).

## 8. Brand and policy alignment (needs your decision)
Navi is positioned around exploring "without limits"; Trails is built around verified access and responsible riding. Rule proposed: whenever Navi shows Trails content it also shows the access class, condition and verification date; Trails never labels private or restricted trails as open; marketing for the two products does not imply that Trails trails can be ridden "without limits" (D-16 in doc 13).
